# FT232 sniff in the truck, 2026-11-15 (board in bypass: AVR out, JP1 fitted)

Both lines are 57,600 baud 8N1 (readable, CR-terminated messages; 8E1/8O1 decode to garbage).
The macOS FTDI driver did not report parity/framing errors, so the format comes from readability.

## Pin 3 line (R7 tap, net HEAD_TXD in the design): heavy display traffic, so this is BODY -> HEAD
2982 bytes in 15 s while the radio was in use. Messages split on CR (0x0D):

    18x  4b 81 37 30   'K.70'
    18x  4b 80 30 30   'K.00'
    15x  ff
    13x  4a 80 30 30   'J.00'
    12x  4a 81 31 30   'J.10'
    ~9x each: 5b 41 20 3N 57 58 20 20 20 3N ff 80 80 82 82 80 ... (16 more bytes)  '[A NWX   N....'  for N = 1..10
     2x  4a 81 32 30   'J.20'
     1x  4b 81 32 30   'K.20'

## Pin 4 line (R6 tap, net BODY_TXD in the design): light traffic, so this is HEAD -> BODY
About 56 bytes in 6 s. Examples: 'c4 46 46 0d', 'c4 30 31 0d', 'ff 0d', '89 0d', 'b0 0d', '8a 0d', '81 0d'.

## Conclusion
The data directions are the reverse of the design: the body transmits on pin 3 and the head on pin 4.
Bypass works because it connects 3-3 and 4-4 regardless. In forward mode the head's pin-4 messages
(including power-on) hit the AVR's output and never reach the body.
No brightness messages ('33 3x 0D') were identified in this short sniff; a proper capture is still needed.
