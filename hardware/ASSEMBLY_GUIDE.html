Before soldering

  1. Set up ESD: mat grounded, wrist strap on.Matters most for U1 and U3; habit for everything.
  2. Lay the bare board on the 1:1 print and confirm it matches.
  3. Bare-board continuity: J1 to J2, pin for pin.
    Expected
    Pins 1, 2, 3 (J1 ↔ J2)≈ 0 Ω each
    Pin 4 (J1 ↔ J2)open
    PB (R1 left pad) ↔ TP2open, no short
    Pin 1 is the square pad on each jack footprint (see map).
  4. Dry-fit J1, J2, J3, the DIP socket and C6.Jacks and J3 should drop in with pegs seated. C6's 1.5 mm leads need a slight spread to match the 2.0 mm holes; spread them now.
  5. Sort ceramics: 104 (C1, C2, C3, C5) apart from 150 (C7, C8).
  6. Keep each resistor value in its own DigiKey bag until its soldering step.Each step in section 5 names its bag. Meter every resistor as it comes out.
5

Soldering, lowest parts first

Every joint: tin the tip, heat pad and lead together for 1–2 s, feed solder into the joint (not onto the tip), remove solder, then iron. Aim for a shiny concave cone. Bend leads out 30° on the underside to hold parts while you solder; trim later. Ground-pour pins (jack pin 2, socket 15 and 21, U2 GND, C4/C6 −, J4 pin 1, capacitor GND pads) take longer: raise to about 350–360 °C (660–680 °F) if solder balls instead of wetting.
One bag per step. Each step uses one DigiKey bag (MPN shown). Open only that bag, meter each resistor as it comes out, and close the bag before the next step.
  1. 10 kΩ ¼ W: R6, R7, R11. MFR-25FBF52-10K · meter 9.9–10.1 k All three lie flat on the board, like any resistor: bend both leads down 90° so they're 10.16 mm apart, and press the body against the board. R6 and R7 run left to right. R11 is the same but turned to run up and down, along the right edge, just right of C8 and TP2.
  2. 470 Ω ¼ W: R2, R13. MFR-25FBF52-470R · meter 465–475 Both lie flat (10.16 mm pitch). Its bands look like the 680 Ω's; trust the meter.
  3. 680 Ω ¼ W: R10. MFR-25FBF52-680R · meter 673–687 7.62 mm pitch: bend its leads tight against the body ends. Inside the ILL zone.
  4. 2.2 kΩ ½ W: R9. MFR50SFTE52-2K2 · meter 2.18–2.22 k Larger body. Inside the ILL zone. Keep the bag: a spare goes into the programmer in section 9.
  5. 150 Ω 1 W: R1. FMP100JR-52-150R · meter 143–158 Largest body. Lift it about 1 mm off the board so it can shed heat.
  6. D1 1N4148. 1N4148 (onsemi)
    ⚠️ Polarity. Cathode band to the LEFT, on the square pad at x 39.4, matching the band on the silk.
  7. Y1 crystal, lying flat with the can toward the top edge. AB26TRB-32.768KHZ-T
    ⚠️ Fragile. Bend each lead gently about 1 mm from the can, never at the glass seal. Quick joints: flux, tinned tip, 1–2 s per lead, 10 s between leads. Keep the iron at the normal setting; a cooler tip means longer heat.
    The leads are very short and may not come through the board. Tape the can down to hold it and solder on the top side, where the leads enter the pads (the holes are plated, so a top joint is fine). Keep the tip off the can, and don't trim the leads. Don't pre-tin these pads: solder fills the 0.5 mm holes and is hard to remove.
  8. Check Y1's joints with the meter (continuity). Probe the lead wire itself, between the can and the pad, so the reading goes through the joint. The socket isn't fitted yet, so its holes are bare pads.
    Expected

    Left lead ↔ socket pin 17 (top row, 3rd from right)≈ 0 Ω
    Y1 pad ↔ Y1 padopen

    The socket falls out when the board is flipped: put a book or cardboard over it, flip both together, and let the board rest on the socket (or tape it). Tack two opposite corners, pin 28 (top-left) and pin 14 (bottom-right); neither is on the ground pour, so they're quick. Check it sits flat; reheat a tack and press if not. Then solder the remaining 26. Pins 15 and 21 are on the ground pour and may need 660–680 °F.
  10. U3 SFH617A-3, soldered directly (no socket). SFH617A-3
    ⚠️ Orientation. Pin-1 dot at TOP-RIGHT, on the square pad nearest the board's right edge. The chip's two rows of legs run left to right (pins 2 and 1 on top, 3 and 4 below).
  11. 100 nF ceramics (104): C1, C2, C3, C5. C320C104K5R5TA Not polarized. C1 is beside JP1, C2 beside the socket's right end, C3 below the socket's left half, C5 next to C6.
  12. 15 pF ceramics (150): C7, C8. C315C150J1G5TA Not polarized. They're the pair just right of the socket's top-right corner, not right next to the crystal. Check the 150 marking before each one.
  13. JP1 (1×2) and J4 (1×3), snapped from the 40-pin strip. PRPC040SAAN-RC Solder one pin, check the header is square to the board, then the rest. Park the JP1 shunt on one pin only.
  14. U2 regulator (TO-92). LM2936Z-5.0/NOPB
    ⚠️ Orientation. Flat face toward the board centre (away from the bottom edge), matching the flat line on the silk. OUT is the square pad on the right, IN on the left.
    If you're fitting the AP7381-50V-A, check its datasheet pinout first. The BOM claims 1 = OUT, 2 = GND, 3 = IN, the same as the LM2936. If it differs, stop and ask Claude.
  15. C4 22 µF tantalum. T350F226K016AT
    ⚠️ Polarity. + lead in the square pad (left, + on silk). A reversed tantalum fails hard, sometimes with smoke.
  16. C6 10 µF electrolytic. EEA-FC1E100

    The case assumes MICROFIT_H = 10.3 mm. Write down your reading: ______ mm. If it differs, tell Claude to regenerate the case before you print.
  18. J1 BODY and J2 HEAD, last. 615004143821 Pegs in, press the faces flush with the board edge, solder one pin each and re-check flush before the rest.
    ⚠️ Bridges. The four pins are staggered and close. Inspect every gap with a loupe; wick any bridge.
6

Trim, clean, inspect

  1. Trim all leads to about 1 mm.The case allows 4 mm under the board. Set aside a spare 2.2 kΩ (R9 value) for the programmer in section 9.
  2. Clean flux with 90%+ IPA (99% preferred) and the brush, then dry fully. Not water: rosin and tack-flux residue won't wash off with it.Pay most attention to the ILL zone at the top right: residue there undermines the isolation.
  3. Glue Y1's can to the board once the board is clean and dry.A small dab of neutral-cure RTV silicone or hot glue between the can and the board, so vibration doesn't work the short leads. Avoid acid-cure (vinegar-smelling) silicone. The datasheet asks for this.
  4. Inspect every joint for bridges, cold joints and missing solder.
  5. Meter PB (R1 left lead) to TP2 again, and the socket's pin 20 to pin 21 (5 V to GND).
    ExpectedNeither reads near 0 Ω. Capacitors charging from the meter will make the reading climb; that's fine.

J3 plug

  1. Take a FEMALE 2×1 housing from the kit and test-fit it on J3.It should click and come off only when you press the latch.

Tests with the AVR out

AVR already programmed? Tests C and E also work with U1 in and the forward-only firmware running (JP1 parked); only J2 pin 4 reads differently (≈ 5 V). Test D, the bypass, always needs U1 out. See section 0 for the order.

U1 stays out of its socket for every test in this section (unless the note above applies). Socket pin numbers below are counted on the socket: pin 1 bottom-left, 1–14 along the bottom row left to right, 15–28 along the top row right to left.

A · Power from the bench supply

  1. Set the bench supply to 10 V, current limit about 30 mA, output off.10 V stands in for the radio's PB. Don't use 12–14 V to mimic the truck.
  2. + to R1's left lead (PB end), − to TP2. Output on.
    Expected
    Supply current< 1 mA
    Socket pin 20 to pin 215.0 V
    Socket pins 6 and 14 to GND5.0 V
    STOPIf the supply hits its current limit, or 5 V is missing or wrong: output off. Check U2 orientation, C4 and C6 polarity, and bridges near the power section.

B · Lights input

  1. Keep A connected. Connect J3 pin 1 (ILL+ wire) to the supply +, J3 pin 2 to −. Measure socket pin 7 to GND.
    Expected
    ILL powered< 0.4 V
    ILL disconnected≈ 5 V
    Supply current rises by about 3 mA while ILL is powered.
  2. Disconnect the bench supply completely.

C · Radio, body cable only

  1. Plug only the body cable into J1. Head cable stays out. Radio on.
  2. Measure each J2 pin to GND (TP2) at its solder joint.
    Expected on J2
    Pin 1 (PB)≈ 10 V
    Pin 2 (GND)0 V
    Pin 3 (data line, body → head)≈ 5 V
    Pin 4 (data line, head → body)≈ 0 V or floating (U1 out) · ≈ 5 V (U1 in, programmed)
    Socket pin 20 to 215.0 V
    STOPIf 10 V appears on any pin other than pin 1, unplug the radio and don't connect the head. Check the jacks and cable before anything else.

D · Bypass with the head

  1. Radio off. Fit the JP1 shunt (U1 still out). Plug the head cable into J2. Radio on.
    ExpectedThe radio works normally: display, keys, brightness. This is a pure bypass.
    ⚠️ JP1 rule. Fit the JP1 shunt only with U1 removed. With the AVR running it fights the body's data line.

E · Protocol capture

  1. Same state as D (or U1 in with the forward-only firmware, JP1 parked). Connect the logic analyzer.
    AnalyzerBoard pointSignal
    GNDTP2 or J4 pin 1Ground
    CH0R6 right lead (x 13.16)TAP_BODY, body → head
    CH1R7 right lead (x 26.16)TAP_HEAD, head → body
    The 10 kΩ in series limits current into the analyzer. With U1 out, each side of the resistor carries the same signal.
  2. PulseView: sample at 1–2 MHz. Add a UART decoder on each channel: 57,600 baud, 8N1, idle high, hex output.
  3. Record: radio power-on; key presses; stepping brightness through the menu; a minute of idle.
    Look forBrightness messages 33 3x 0D on CH0, where 3x is 30–38 (ASCII '0'–'8').
    Framing errors? The baud rate is wrong. Measure the narrowest bit width with the cursors: 17.4 µs means 57,600.
  4. Save the capture (.sr) and ask Claude to review it.This confirms the protocol constants before full firmware goes in.

DIY SerialUPDI programmer

One CH340 module and a 2.2 kΩ resistor stand in for a UPDI Friend.

  1. Set the CH340 jumper to 5V. Plug it in and measure TX to GND.
    ExpectedTX idles at ≈ 5 V.
  2. Build the breadboard as drawn: 2.2 kΩ (a spare R9) between the TX row and the RX row.
  3. RX row to J4 pin 2 (M-F jumper). Adapter GND to J4 pin 1 (F-F jumper).
    ⚠️ Power. Leave the adapter's VCC/5V pin unconnected. The board is powered by the radio or the bench supply (10 V on R1's left lead), never by the adapter.
If you get "UPDI initialisation failed": first check the adapter's GND really goes to J4 pin 1 (not DTR or CTS next to it). Then run tools/serial_loopback.py PORT with TXD and RXD joined: it must echo cleanly at every speed. The first build's CH340 modules failed this (their receive side didn't work); an FT232 adapter passed and programmed the board. See firmware/PROGRAMMING_HANDOFF.md.
If flashing is flaky: ask Claude to make the slow 57,600 baud mode selectable, or replace the resistor with a small-signal Schottky (BAT43 or BAT85), cathode toward TX. A 1N4148 won't work.

Flash and log

  1. Radio off. Remove the JP1 shunt and park it on one pin.
  2. Straighten the AVR's legs to 0.3 in by rolling each row against the bench. Insert it.
    ⚠️ Orientation. AVR notch to the LEFT, matching the socket. Wrist strap on.
  3. Connect the programmer (section 9), body and head cables. Radio on. Find the port.
    ls /dev/cu.*      # /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX or /dev/cu.wchusbserial…
  4. Set fuses, once per new chip.
    make fuses PORT=/dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX
    Sets 1 ms startup, 2.85 V brown-out, and keeps EEPROM across reflashes.
  5. Flash the forward-only firmware.
    make flash-forward PORT=/dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX
    ExpectedThe radio behaves exactly as in bypass (test D).
    STOPIf the display freezes or garbles, pull U1, fit the JP1 shunt and ask Claude before going further.
  6. Logging: the FT232 (jumper on 5V). RX to J4 pin 3, GND to J4 pin 1, nothing on TX.Both adapters share J4 pin 1: bring it to a breadboard GND row and connect both GNDs there.
    ⚠️ RX and GND only. Leave the logging adapter's TXD unconnected, and don't reuse the programmer's row B with its TXD still feeding the 2.2 kΩ. An idle-high TXD back-powers the AVR through its pin whenever the board's supply is off, and the log fills with partial KP lines from brown-out resets.
    make log LOGPORT=/dev/cu.usbserial-A50285BI
  7. Full firmware, only after Claude confirms the protocol constants from your capture.
    make flash PORT=/dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX
  8. Verify: set brightness by day, toggle ILL (bench supply on J3), set it by night, cycle radio power. Watch the log.

11

Enclosure

⚠️ Firmware first. J4 and JP1 are sealed inside the case. Finish flashing and logging before you close it.
  1. J3 height confirmed (or case regenerated). Print base and cover in PETG or ASA.
  2. Set the board on the base standoffs and lower the cover over the connectors.
  3. Drive 4 × M3 × 16 from below into the 12 mm pilots (about 8.4 mm of engagement).They cut their own thread in plastic: snug, then stop.
  4. Optional: run it in the case on the bench for an hour with the logger off, then check that nothing is warm and the radio still behaves.
  5. Install: body cable to J1, head cable to J2, ILL plug to J3.
Recovery
  • Hard bypass: pull U1 and fit the JP1 shunt. The radio runs as if the board weren't there.
  • Known-good coupler: a spare board fitted with only J1, J2 and a JP1 shunt.
  • Store-bought RJ couplers: many are reversed (1→4), which puts PB on the head's RX pin. Check continuity pin for pin before using one.
